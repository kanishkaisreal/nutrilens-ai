# CarbKind AI Product Principles

These principles keep CarbKind focused on useful food decisions rather than unsupported certainty or unnecessary interaction.

## Useful approximation over fragile precision

Meal images rarely reveal exact recipes, weights, or portions. CarbKind should provide honest ranges, name uncertainty, and prioritize what a user can do next.

## No hardware dependency

The core experience should work with a phone camera and ordinary ingredient input. Food scales, wearables, and special sensors may add context later, but they must not be required.

## No fake exactness

Do not present an image estimate as a laboratory measurement. Prefer rough nutrition ranges, likely ingredients, and the main carb source over false decimal-level precision.

## Minimal questions

Infer what can be inferred safely. Ask only when one answer would materially improve the recommendation, such as available time, a dietary constraint, or confirmation of an inferred preference.

## One recommendation first, details second

Lead with the most useful action: one plate observation, one better swap, or one meal suggestion. Keep supporting details available without making them the first thing a user must parse.

## Healthy food should not be boring

Food choices must remain satisfying and culturally flexible. Improving a meal should preserve the reason someone wanted to eat it.

## Preserve flavor while improving balance

Substitutions should respect taste, texture, cuisine, and ingredient function while improving the balance of carbohydrates, protein, fiber, and vegetables where practical.

## Photos are useful beyond calorie estimation

A meal photo can reveal recurring ingredients, cuisine patterns, preparation styles, and food preferences. These signals can support better future decisions without pretending the photo provides exact nutrition.

## Personalization must be consented

No meal should become memory by default. Users should understand what is retained and have clear controls to review, export, correct, or delete it.

## Confirm inferred preferences

An observed pattern is not automatically a rule. CarbKind should ask users to confirm inferred preferences before treating them as strict constraints.

## Mobile-first before native app

Validate the camera, meal-result, and composition flows in a responsive mobile-first web experience before taking on native-app complexity.
